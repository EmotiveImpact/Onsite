# Heron Reach foliage kit

Real modelled trees, grass, weeds, shrubs, hedges and leaf litter for the Heron Reach estate:
late autumn at blue hour, wet, partly bare London planes and silver birches. Everything in the kit
is original work, generated by the scripts listed below. Nothing was downloaded, traced or adapted
from third-party assets.

## Files

| what | where |
| --- | --- |
| Blender source (one collection per asset, images packed) | `assets/blender/heron-reach/foliage-kit.blend` |
| Manifest (triangles, bounds, collision, LODs, texture provenance) | `assets/blender/heron-reach/foliage-manifest.json` |
| Textures (WebP, 1024 x 1024) | `public/materials/heron/foliage/` |
| Texture generator (numpy and Pillow) | `scripts/maps/art/make_heron_foliage_textures.py` |
| Shared atlas layout (cell rectangles and pivots) | `scripts/maps/art/heron_foliage_atlas.py` |
| Blender builder | `scripts/maps/art/make_heron_foliage.py` |
| Preview renders (Cycles CPU) | `scripts/maps/art/render_heron_foliage.py` |
| GLB export test | `scripts/maps/art/export_heron_foliage_test.py` |

Rebuild, in order:

```
python3 scripts/maps/art/make_heron_foliage_textures.py
python3 scripts/maps/art/make_heron_foliage.py
python3 scripts/maps/art/render_heron_foliage.py            # optional previews
python3 scripts/maps/art/export_heron_foliage_test.py OUT   # optional GLB check
```

Every generator uses a fixed seed, so a rebuild gives the same textures and meshes. The builder
replaces the whole `.blend`; make manual edits in a copy or in the scripts.

## Assets

All origins sit at the base centre on Z = 0, transforms are applied and one Blender unit is one
metre. Triangle counts and exact bounds are in the manifest.

| collection | contents | triangles |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `TREE_PLANE_A`, `TREE_PLANE_B` | London plane, about 8.0 m and 7.5 m | about 9.6k and 9.0k |
| `TREE_BIRCH_A`, `TREE_BIRCH_B` | silver birch, about 7.0 m and 6.1 m | about 8.3k and 6.8k |
| `<TREE>_LOD1` | reduced tree in `HR_FOLIAGE_LOD1` | 1.6k to 2.7k |
| `GRASS_CLUMP_A` to `D` | 11 to 28 curved blades, 0.12 to 0.45 m | 84 to 196 |
| `GRASS_PATCH_1M` | 1 x 1 m lawn tile: 25 clumps plus 60 low crossed tuft cards | about 4.4k |
| `GRASS_EDGE_1M` | sparse 1 m verge and kerb strip | about 1.0k |
| `WEED_TUFT_A` to `C` | dandelion rosette, paving-crack grass, wall-base herb | 36 to 66 |
| `SHRUB_A` to `C` | 0.5, 0.5 and 0.7 m evergreen shrubs | 1.6k to 1.9k |
| `HEDGE_1M`, `HEDGE_END` | clipped privet, 1.0 x 0.6 x 1.0 m | about 1k each |
| `LEAF_LITTER_A` to `C` | 1 x 1 m fallen leaves on Z = 0.005: gutter drift, heaps, sparse | 480 to 1.8k |

Each tree has two objects, `<TREE>_bark` and `<TREE>_leaves`, so the bark and alpha-clipped leaves
export as separate glTF primitives. Shrubs and hedges are single objects with two materials.

### Clearance and collision

- Tree trunks are clear of branches and leaves up to at least 2.75 m (the manifest records the
  lowest leaf vertex for each tree, all above 2.79 m). Use the manifest cylinder for the trunk:
  radius 0.19 to 0.27 m, from 0 to 2 m. The root flare is wider at ground level but is cosmetic.
  Canopies have no collision.
- Hedges block players: give each module an explicit 1.0 x 0.6 x 1.0 m box collider.
- Grass, weeds, shrubs (all at or below 0.7 m) and litter are walk-through.

### Tiling

- `HEDGE_1M` is open at both ends and tiles every 1.0 m along X. `HEDGE_END` is open at -X and
  closed and rounded at +X; rotate it 180 degrees for the other end of a run.
- `GRASS_PATCH_1M` covers a 1 m cell. Place it on a 1 m grid with random 90 degree turns over the
  `hr_grass_ground` texture; blades spill slightly over the cell edge to hide the seams.

## Algorithms

**Trees: space colonisation.** Attraction points fill a species crown envelope: a union of
limb-scale blobs (each major limb carries its own rounded mass, which gives the lumpy plane
outline) plus a noise-warped ovoid (planes) or a narrow tapering oval (birches), thinned by a 3D
clumping noise. A straight trunk grows to about 2.6 m, then a leader continues up the crown (to
about 4.5 m for planes, near the top for birches) and may sprout laterals once it is above the
clearance height. Each iteration, every attraction point pulls its nearest growing node; nodes step
0.14 to 0.15 m towards the mean pull plus a phototropism bias; points within the kill distance are
removed. Radii follow the pipe model (r^n of a parent equals the sum over its children), with the
exponent solved so the trunk is 0.22 to 0.23 m (planes) or 0.17 m (birches) just above the flare.
A gravity pass then lets thin wood sag in proportion to its slenderness, strongly for the
pendulous birch twigs. Branch wood that would drop below 2.78 m is pruned.

**Bark meshes.** The node tree splits into chains that follow the thickest child. Chains are
smoothed, resampled at a radius-dependent spacing and swept as tapered tubes with parallel
transport frames, 3 to 16 sides by radius, closed to a point at the twig ends. UVs are cylindrical
and continuous: U wraps once (or more on very thick wood) around the branch with duplicated seam
vertices, V follows arc length at the same texel scale. The trunk has five buttress lobes in the
root flare and a little noise in its section. Custom normals are radial so the seam does not show.

**Bark colour.** Both bark materials multiply the texture by a vertex colour (`Col`, exported as
glTF `COLOR_0`). On birches it turns thin twigs dark red-brown and gives the lower trunk patchy
black fissured bark; on planes it darkens the twigs and the soil splash at the base.

**Leaves.** Leaf cards sit on twig nodes thinner than 12 to 16 mm, above the crown base. A
low-frequency 3D noise sets a per-region density so some parts of the crown are bare and others
clustered; twig tips are favoured. Plane cards are mostly three to five leaf twiglet clusters
(0.42 to 0.55 m) with some single leaves; birch cards are smaller and hang from the twig. Cards
are bent slightly under gravity and carry custom normals blended towards the canopy's radial
direction so the crown lights as a soft volume. Leaves stop at least 2.85 m above the ground.

**LOD1.** The same skeleton with twigs thinner than 8 mm dropped, fewer sides, longer segments,
60 percent of the cards at 1.45 times the size and flat (two triangle) cards.

**Grass.** Each blade is a four-segment tapered strip that leans and bends progressively and twists
slightly, mapped to one of twelve atlas columns so colour and dried-tip length vary blade to blade.
Normals lean towards +Z so lawns light like the ground.

**Weeds, shrubs, hedges, litter.** Rosette leaves are short bent strips radiating from the centre;
crack and wall weeds are crossed cards with a few real blades. Shrubs grow a small recursive twig
structure and layer leaf cards on a mound envelope, attached to the nearest twig. Hedges hide a
dark opaque core box (mapped to an inner-foliage tile in the atlas) behind about 400 sprig cards
on the front, back and top, with a few stems visible at the base. Litter leaves are folded 3 x 3
vertex quads lying on Z = 0.005, distributed as a gutter drift, small heaps or a sparse scatter.

## Textures and materials

| material | textures | how they were made |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `hr_leaf_plane` | `plane-leaf-atlas-color` (with alpha), `-normal` | palmate 3 to 5 lobed outline from a soft maximum of pointed polar lobes with coarse teeth; primary and secondary veins as distance fields; yellow-green to tan and brown with blotches and browned margins |
| `hr_leaf_birch` | `birch-leaf-atlas-color`, `-normal` | deltoid-ovate outline, acuminate tip, double serration, seven straight vein pairs; yellow-green to gold |
| `hr_bark_plane` | `plane-bark-color`, `-normal`, `-orm` | two scales of periodic, domain-warped Worley cells, each plate given a bark age (cream, olive, grey-green, grey-brown) with smaller flakes peeled to younger layers; rain streaks and algae tint |
| `hr_bark_birch` | `birch-bark-color`, `-normal`, `-orm` | cream-white base, grey mottling, pinkish peeled bands, 300 wrapped horizontal lenticels, ragged dark fissured patches |
| `hr_grass_blade` | `grass-blades-color`, `-normal` | twelve blade columns in damp dark greens with keeled midribs, striations and ragged dried straw tips; two side-view tuft cards |
| `hr_weed` | `weeds-color`, `-normal` | runcinate dandelion leaves, plantain leaves with parallel veins, a seeding crack grass and a groundsel-type sprig |
| `hr_leaf_litter` | `leaf-litter-color`, `-normal` | sixteen fallen plane and birch leaves in wet browns with dark spotting, holes and curl |
| `hr_hedge_leaf` | `hedge-leaf-atlas-color`, `-normal` | privet sprigs with opposite small glossy leaves, an opaque inner-foliage tile, small sprigs |
| `hr_shrub_leaf` | `shrub-leaf-atlas-color`, `-normal` | laurel-type oval leaves with a few bronze older leaves |

All textures are drawn at twice the resolution and box-filtered down; atlas colour is bled under
transparent pixels so mipmaps do not fringe. Normal maps are derived from height maps in the OpenGL
convention. Bark textures tile in both directions, one tile per metre. ORM follows glTF (R occlusion,
G roughness, B metal 0).

Materials are Principled BSDF. Alpha-cut materials pass the image alpha through a Math Round node
and have backface culling off, which the glTF exporter writes as `alphaMode: MASK` (cutoff 0.5) and
`doubleSided: true`. Bark occlusion goes through the `glTF Material Output` group.

## Export check

`export_heron_foliage_test.py` exported `TREE_PLANE_A` (about 2.2 MB) and `GRASS_PATCH_1M` (about
0.4 MB) to GLB with WebP images. The leaf and grass materials came out as `MASK`, double-sided,
with all textures embedded in the binary chunk via `EXT_texture_webp`; the bark primitive carries
`COLOR_0`. Image format `AUTO` gives the same result because the sources are WebP.

## Limits

- Principled BSDF has no thin-leaf translucency, so in Cycles previews the shaded side of a leaf
  card is dark. In the game, the radial custom normals do most of this work; a translucency or
  wrap-lighting term in the leaf shader would help further.
- Previews are Cycles CPU stills on a stand-in set (gradient sky, plain wet ground, one warm lamp).
  They show the look of the assets, not in-engine performance.
- Tree triangle counts are within budget, but crowns are alpha heavy (about 850 to 1,100 cards).
  Measure overdraw in the browser before placing many trees close together.
# Winter render variant — 19 October 2026

`winter-trees.blend` / `winter-manifest.json` contain the existing four original ONSITE
deterministic plane and birch skeletons, with 4% of the autumn leaf-card count. They use this
document's existing procedural bark/leaf textures. Runtime render replacement only: the original
foliage kit, placement scale and collider manifest are preserved. Blender 4.3.2; metre scale,
ground-centred pivots; glTF Y-up export. No external asset or licence is introduced.

```sh
HR_FOLIAGE_WINTER=1 HR_FOLIAGE_ONLY=TREE HR_FOLIAGE_BLEND="$PWD/assets/blender/heron-reach/winter-trees.blend" HR_FOLIAGE_MANIFEST="$PWD/assets/blender/heron-reach/winter-manifest.json" blender -b --python-exit-code 1 --python scripts/maps/art/make_heron_foliage.py
blender -b --python-exit-code 1 --python scripts/maps/art/export_heron_kits_glb.py -- winter
npx gltfpack -i public/models/heron-reach/heron-reach-winter.raw.glb -o public/models/heron-reach/heron-reach-winter.glb -cc -kn -km -kv -vtf
```
